# Stadler: Steel vs Aluminium carbodies

Back to [[00 Index]] · Log: [[Projects/Stadler – Steel vs Aluminium/Decisions|Decisions]] · Sources: [[Sources/Summary – Stadler case deck]], [[Sources/Summary – Sponsor talks transcript#Stadler]]

## Goal
Work out the life-cycle energy cost of two FLIRT 5-car EMUs: **Train A** (aluminium, lighter, 400,000 PLN more expensive) and **Train B** (steel, heavier). Then decide whether paying more upfront is worth it, and whether the answer still holds in Kazakhstan.

Required outputs (5 parts):
1. Single train: annual energy saving, annual cost saving, 30-year total.
2. Fleet of 20: 30-year energy saving, average annual saving, cumulative saving.
3. Payback period for the extra 400k PLN, and the net benefit over 30 years.
4. Scenario with electricity prices rising 2% a year: single train, fleet, effect on payback.
5. **Analytical conclusion.** This is the part judging is based on. It covers:
   - key findings
   - whether tenders should use life-cycle cost
   - how much of this transfers to Kazakhstan
   - which Kazakhstan factors raise or lower energy use
   - what extra data to request from the manufacturer and the customer

## Audience
Stadler Kazakhstan judges: a manager plus the chief welder (Daulet Absatov), who's available for technical questions. This is Stadler's first hackathon and they're partly using it to recruit.

## Constraints
- One-day event. More detail is promised in the Q&A session.
- Scope is **operating energy only**: no manufacturing, disposal, or full life-cycle assessment / life-cycle cost.
- The input data contradicts itself. Confirm in Q&A before relying on it:
  - Aluminium train weight: 145 t on the slide vs 155 t in the talk.
  - Electricity price: 1.2 **USD**/kWh for Train A vs 1.2 **PLN**/kWh for Train B.
  - "Energy savings 357 MWh/yr" is listed under both trains, probably meaning the difference between them.
- Stadler's Kazakhstan product is locomotive-hauled sleeping coaches, not EMUs. Energy savings in Kazakhstan may come from diesel on non-electrified lines.

## Status
**Recommended #1 of 3. Waiting for the team to confirm.**

Working baseline, assuming 357 MWh/yr at 1.2 PLN/kWh:
- About 428k PLN saved per train per year, so the premium pays back in about 0.9 years.
- About 12.9M PLN per train over 30 years (about 257M for the fleet of 20). With 2% annual price growth: about 17.4M per train (about 348M for the fleet).
- Rough Kazakhstan check at about $0.07/kWh (tariff not yet verified): payback stretches to about 4 years.

Next steps: confirm the data in Q&A → build the spreadsheet model → research Kazakhstan tariffs and the share of non-electrified lines → write Part 5.
