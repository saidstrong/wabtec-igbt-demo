# Summary: Stadler case deck

**Original:** [[Originals/Stadler_Kazakhstan_NU_171026.pptx]] · **Project:** [[Projects/Stadler – Steel vs Aluminium/Brief|Stadler brief]]

**Company:** Stadler Kazakhstan LLP, set up in 2023 in Astana. It has a 31,000 m² plant, a KZT 29.5 bn investment, 650+ staff, and 557 cars to deliver to KTZ under a 2022 contract (26.4 m long, up to 160 km/h, rated −50 to +45 °C).

**Case: energy costs of steel vs aluminium carbodies, using FLIRT trains.**

| | Train A | Train B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body | Aluminium | Steel |
| Mass | ~145 t | ~180 t |
| Price | +400,000 PLN vs B | baseline |

Both: 5-car EMU, 15 stops, 140 km/h top speed, 245,000 km/yr, "energy savings 357 MWh/yr per train". The price is 1.2 USD/kWh for A and 1.2 PLN/kWh for B.

**Tasks:**
1. Single train: annual energy saving, annual cost saving, 30-year saving.
2. Fleet of 20: 30-year energy saving, average annual saving, cumulative saving.
3. Payback on the 400k PLN premium and the net benefit over 30 years.
4. 2%/yr tariff increase: single train, fleet, effect on payback.
5. Analytical conclusion:
   - key findings
   - should tenders use life-cycle operating cost?
   - how well does this transfer to Kazakhstan?
   - which Kazakhstan factors raise or lower consumption?
   - what extra data to ask the manufacturer and customer for?

**Method notes:** based on a real case, with the cost difference adjusted. Only operating energy counts: no production, manufacturing, or end-of-life. Without a full life-cycle assessment or life-cycle cost study, the total environmental impact can't be determined.

> [!warning] Data issues
> USD vs PLN mismatch; 357 MWh repeated under both trains; the talk said 155 t, not 145 t.
